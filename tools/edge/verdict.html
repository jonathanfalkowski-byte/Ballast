Edge experiment · NQ + MNQ · Jan–Aug 2026

Do the dots work?

252 sessions of MNQ and real NQ tick data, 1,008 configurations tested. The two original setups: no. One late-arriving variant — dots filtered by a Bollinger extreme — survived a sealed holdout and earned the right to a forward test. It is not an edge yet.

Not near it. Inside it — the strongest variant sits at z = +0.53 against the random baseline, the weakest at z = −1.60. There is no separation to find.

The whole finding, in one chart

What was actually tested

The dot is Quantum Vol-Delta by ninZa.co. I did not have its source, so I rebuilt it from the parameters in your indicator panel: volume base BidAskPrice_RealVolume, size filter 3–999, delta period 20, strong/moderate ratio 3. That becomes |bar delta| ÷ SMA(|delta|, 20) — ratio ≥ 3 prints strong, ≥ 1 prints moderate.

Your tick exports carry the bid and ask at the moment of each trade, so delta here is real aggressor classification — trades lifting the offer versus hitting the bid — not the uptick/downtick guess most backtests settle for.

Two checks before trusting any of it

  • Delta sign matches bar direction 80.9% of the time. If it were 50% the classification would be broken; if it were 100% the dot would just be a repaint of the candle. 81% is what real order flow looks like.
  • Random entries reproduce theory. A driftless random walk with a 135-tick stop and 141-tick target should win 135 ÷ 276 = 48.91%. The random control came in at 48.60%. The simulator measures what it claims to measure.

The part that explains why

Remove the bracket entirely and just ask which way price goes after a signal fires. Negative means the signal pointed the wrong way.

Ticks moved in the signal's own direction. Negative at nearly every horizon, for every variant. The strongest readings — A·strong at −24.7 ticks and B·strong at −15.7 ticks at 15 minutes — carry the clearest statistical signature.

That pattern has a plain reading: these are momentum entries arriving late. A delta surge large enough to print a dot is the move already happening. Entering on it means buying from the people who are finishing, and price mean-reverts against you.

Why fading it is not the answer either

The obvious next thought is to flip every signal. I tested it, because you would have asked. Best fade variant: +$1.40 per trade at t = +0.71. That is noise wearing a profitable face.

Then I widened it — 720 configurations across both setups, three bar sizes, two delta series, three dot thresholds, five stop/target brackets, follow and fade:

  • 82 of 720 came back profitable (11.4%) — about what pure chance produces when you look 720 times.
  • Best result: t = +2.07. The Bonferroni threshold for 720 tests is |t| > 3.98. It is not close.
  • Every top performer is a fade, clustered in one thin corner of the grid (60-second bars, filtered delta, strong dots only, n = 249). One narrow pocket winning while its neighbours lose is the signature of overfitting, not of an edge.
  • Mean t across all 720 = −2.01. Not an inverse edge — that is transaction cost becoming statistically visible once the trade count gets large.

Chasing that fade would be the exact move this experiment exists to prevent: abandoning a tested answer for an untested hunch, and resetting the sample to zero again.

The Bollinger filter, and why it is the most useful result here

A reasonable objection: maybe the dots need context. Only take them at a Bollinger extreme, after price has tagged the band and turned back. That is a real hypothesis with a mechanism — if the dots mark exhaustion rather than continuation, a band extreme is exactly where exhaustion should appear.

So it was tested properly: 144 configurations developed on Jan–Mar only, with Apr–Jun sealed off until a single configuration was locked.

In-sample, it looked like a discovery. Best configuration +$8.62 per trade at t = +2.53; one variant reached +$10.69 per trade. 57 of 144 configurations profitable. Stop reading here and you have found an edge.

Out-of-sample, all of it vanished. The locked configuration went from +$8.62 to −$4.46 per trade, its win rate falling from 45.4% to 30.9%. Of the top five, the best survivor managed +$0.37 per trade at t = +0.13.

The best defense, tested and failed

Mean-reversion setups genuinely do struggle in trends, so the fair objection is that Apr–Jun was simply the wrong regime. Two checks:

  • Reverse the split. Train on Apr–Jun, validate on Jan–Mar. Only 2 of 5 carried over, the best at t = +1.03 — noise. Training on that half produced 39 of 144 profitable, mean t = −0.62.
  • Compare the regimes directly. Inside 9:30–12:00 the halves are near-identical: mean net move 700 vs 636 ticks, session range 1,229 vs 1,268 ticks, directional bias +78 vs +57 ticks. The contract rallied 26% over the period, but the window being traded did not become meaningfully trendier.

There was no regime shift for the filter to be a victim of. It fit noise in one half and did not transfer to the other, in both directions.

A filter that produces +$10.69 per trade on the data you built it from and −$6.41 on data you did not is the exact shape of every indicator pack and every promising tweak that has ever looked convincing on a chart. The in-sample number is real. It is just not evidence.

That was the verdict on MNQ. Then the NQ data arrived and the picture changed.

The NQ rerun, and one correction

Everything above ran on MNQ because it was the only tick data available. The largest stated caveat was that the dots are computed on NQ order flow. That caveat is now closed: real NQ tick data, 142 sessions, Jan–Aug 2026.

A correction first. This document previously argued that the vendor's size ≥ 3 filter keeps a large share of NQ volume against only 9% of MNQ, making MNQ a poor proxy. That was asserted without data, and it was wrong. NQ is more one-lot dominated than MNQ — 96.4% of NQ trades are single contracts, and the filter keeps 8.0% of NQ volume against 9.0% of MNQ. The proxy was sound.

Setups A and B on real NQ flow: unchanged. All eight variants land inside the random band (−$5.59 to +$0.85). The best of them, filtered-delta strong dots, returns −$0.34 per trade at t = −0.09 — a coin flip paying commission. The original verdict was not an artifact of the wrong instrument.

The one thing that survived

The NQ export included Jul 1 – Aug 19, a block no test in this project had ever touched. That made a real three-way protocol possible: search on Jan–Mar, check on Apr–Jun, then spend the sealed block once on a single locked configuration.

The locked configuration was C1 · 60s · filtered delta · strong dots · BB(20, 2.5) · 100/100 — tag the band, close back inside, take the first confirming dot.

What argues for it

  • Permutation test, p = 0.035. Holding entry times and trade count fixed and randomising only the direction 200 times, the real result beat 193 shuffles. Random direction at those same moments loses −$2.26, so the direction call is worth roughly +$5.34 against its proper baseline.
  • The parameters sit on a plateau, not a spike. Band width 2.0 to 3.0 returns +$1.93 / +$4.33 / +$3.08 / +$4.52 / +$2.03. Band period 14 to 30 returns +$1.81 through +$3.19. Nothing falls off a cliff.
  • It is bracket-invariant, which is the strongest signal here. Positive at 80/80, 100/100, 120/120, 135/141, 100/150 and 150/100 — and the win rate moves exactly as geometry demands (65.0% at 150/100, 45.2% at 100/150). That is what a genuine directional tilt looks like, not a bracket that happens to fit.

What argues against it

  • It was chosen as the best of 144. Expected number of configurations reaching p < 0.05 by chance alone is about 7. One was found. Bonferroni-corrected, the bar is p < 0.00035.
  • Pooled t = +1.04, and the 95% confidence interval runs −$2.73 to +$8.89. It crosses zero.
  • It lost money for an entire quarter. Apr–Jun at −$4.62 per trade is not what a stable edge does.
  • Bar size is unstable. 30s +$1.58, 45s −$5.67, 60s +$3.08, 90s −$0.41, 120s −$4.87. A real effect should not invert at 45 seconds and return at 60.

And the part nobody wants to hear

Take the result entirely at face value — assume +$3.06 per trade is real. At two trades per session it needs 1,056 trades to reach t = 2. That is 528 sessions, or roughly 25 months of trading, before the edge could be confirmed rather than hoped for.

And it pays $6.12 a session on one MNQ — about $129 a month. The same edge on one full NQ is roughly $1,285 a month, but each stop then risks $500 against a $6,500 trailing buffer, which is 7.7% of the account per trade.

What could still change this

  1. ClosedWrong instrument. Resolved. Real NQ tick data, Jan–Aug 2026, confirms the MNQ verdict on setups A and B, and supplied the sealed holdout for the surviving variant.

  2. OpenThe dot is my reconstruction. It passes both sanity checks and matches the dot density on your chart, but it is not ninZa's formula. If the real one differs, the surviving variant is the result most likely to move.

  3. OpenBar type. Your indicator panel says NQ SEP26 (30 Second); the experiment as recorded says 50 AlgoBars. I tested 15s through 120s. The surviving variant needs 60-second bars and inverts at 45, so bar construction matters more here than anywhere else in this study.

The honest disposition on the surviving variant is not "it works" and not "it does not." It is the one candidate that earned a pre-registered forward test. The only uncontaminated data left is data that has not happened yet. Lock the specification, tag it in Ballast, and let the journal's edge read answer it — which is precisely the machinery this experiment was built around.
