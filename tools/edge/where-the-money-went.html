It is not revenge trading. It is the opposite.

The obvious story would be tilt after losses. The journal says the reverse — you trade your setups best when you are behind.

Your single best bucket in 167 trades is trading your setup while down: 65.2% and +$206 a trade. Your worst by an enormous margin is trading off-setup while up: 9.1% and −$841 a trade, at t = −3.03.

Off-setup trading is poor everywhere — it loses while flat-ish and while down too. But while up it is catastrophic. Losing does not break your discipline nearly as badly as winning does. The notes say it plainly — Afraid to miss it four times, Invincible twice.

Why Ballast did not stop it

The give-back guard is already switched on — *TILT*|1|1 in your settings, which is tilt enabled and give-back enabled. It still did not catch these.

The reason is a timing gap rather than a bug. A give-back wall watches the day fall away from its peak, so it fires after the damage. These seventeen trades are not the aftermath of a give-back — they are the give-back. By the time the wall has something to measure, the money has gone.

The condition that would have caught them is different, and it is already sitting in the fields Ballast records at entry:

  • DailyPnLBefore above roughly $200
  • size above the one contract the plan allows
  • and the trade about to be taken carries no setup — which Ballast cannot know beforehand, but it does not need to. Green day plus over-size catches ten of the eleven on its own.

That is a pre-trade prompt, not a post-hoc wall: you are up $900 on the day and about to click four contracts — which setup is this? If the honest answer is "none", the question has already done its work.

What this means for the dots

A whole day was spent testing whether the vol-delta dots have an edge. The answer was no for setups A and B, and one Bollinger-filtered variant earned a forward test. That work stands — see the companion writeup.

But it was aimed at the wrong target. Setup A returns +$16 a trade and setup B +$24. Even a genuinely good new setup would add a few dollars a trade against a leak running at −$841. No indicator fixes this. No filter, no vendor pack, no better dot.

Ten years, a few hundred accounts, and the thing quietly draining them was never findable on a chart — because it only happens on the days that are going well.
